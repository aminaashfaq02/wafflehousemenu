Cross-contact warning: Waffle House cooks on shared grills and waffle irons, so any item can come into contact with any allergen. This chart shows allergens in the ingredients only. If you have a serious food allergy, confirm with the restaurant before you order.

Things that surprise people

  • The plain waffle lists tree nuts, not just the pecan waffle.
  • Grits list milk, egg, wheat and soy.
  • Regular hashbrowns list soy only.
  • Bacon, sausage, city ham and country ham list no major allergens.
  • Every toast and biscuit lists wheat and milk.

Calories & Allergens FAQ

Waffle House does not offer certified gluten-free food. Some items list no wheat ingredients, including regular hashbrowns, scrambled eggs, bacon, sausage, city ham, country ham and sliced tomatoes, but everything is cooked on a shared grill, so cross-contact with wheat is possible. Grits, toast, biscuits and waffles all list wheat.
Regular hashbrowns list soy as their only major allergen, with no milk ingredients. Ordering them “covered” adds American cheese (milk), and they are cooked on the same grill as cheese and butter, so cross-contact is possible.
Tree nuts are listed for the plain waffle as well as the pecan waffle, and the peanut butter chip waffle also lists peanuts. Anyone with a nut allergy should treat every waffle as containing nuts.
With hashbrowns and white toast, the All-Star Special lists milk, egg, wheat, soy and tree nuts. The waffle brings tree nuts, the toast brings wheat and milk, and the eggs bring egg and soy.
It depends on the order: a two egg breakfast with toast and hashbrowns is about 600 calories, a Texas melt 650–730, and the All-Star Special 1,120–1,270. A regular order of hashbrowns adds 190 calories and toast about 230.
